# Analyzing Renewable-Energy Transitions with R

## A Kenya–Portugal Policy Application

### Research question

How can reproducible quantitative analysis strengthen comparative research on renewable-energy transitions without reducing governance to a set of numbers?

### Purpose

This project develops a practical analytical workflow that combines quantitative analysis in R with comparative policy and institutional research. Quantitative evidence can identify patterns and sharpen policy questions, while qualitative and governance analysis helps explain how and why those patterns emerge.

### Comparative focus

**Kenya** — geothermal development, electricity access, investment, utility performance, renewable deployment, and institutional coordination.

**Portugal / EU** — renewable integration, island systems, EU climate policy, investment, regulation, and multilevel governance.

### Potential indicators

- renewable share of electricity generation
- installed renewable capacity by technology
- investment flows
- electricity prices and affordability
- grid reliability
- access indicators
- emissions intensity
- public and private financing
- policy and regulatory indicators
- macroeconomic controls

### Reproducible workflow

1. Define the policy question and unit of analysis.
2. Build a documented dataset from authoritative public sources.
3. Clean and transform data in R.
4. Produce descriptive trends and comparative visualizations.
5. Select statistical methods appropriate to the question and data.
6. Conduct sensitivity and robustness checks where appropriate.
7. Interpret quantitative results in institutional context.
8. Document limitations and competing explanations.
9. Publish validated scripts, metadata, and figures for replication.

### Interpretation principle

A statistical relationship does not by itself establish a governance mechanism or causal effect. Quantitative analysis should be interpreted together with policy documents, institutional mapping, interviews, and case evidence.

### Possible extensions

- panel-data analysis
- interrupted time-series analysis around policy reforms
- event-study designs where data permit
- investment-risk analysis
- scenario and sensitivity analysis
- comparative visualization of technology pathways

### Teaching value

This project can support future teaching on how to move from a sustainability-policy question to a reproducible analytical workflow and then to a defensible policy interpretation.

### Development approach

R scripts and model specifications will be added only after the underlying data, variable definitions, and code have been tested and documented.
