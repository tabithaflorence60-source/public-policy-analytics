# Africa's Energy Investment Gap to 2030

## Evidence, Finance and Policy

### Research question

What explains the gap between Africa's energy-transition investment needs and actual capital deployment, and which policy arrangements can narrow that gap by 2030?

### Problem

Sub-Saharan Africa combines rapidly growing demand, major energy-access needs, large renewable-resource potential, and persistent infrastructure constraints. The core challenge is therefore not simply technical potential. It is the ability to mobilize, allocate, and implement investment at sufficient scale.

Earlier transition literature estimated very large financing requirements for universal energy access and highlighted the need for major increases in annual power-sector investment. It also emphasized power-sector reform, more effective use of international public finance, and mechanisms to crowd in private capital.

### Analytical pillars

1. **Investment requirement** — generation, transmission, distribution, mini-grids, off-grid systems, storage, and enabling infrastructure.
2. **Financing sources** — domestic public finance, development banks, bilateral finance, private investors, climate funds, and blended structures.
3. **Investment barriers** — utility weakness, currency risk, policy uncertainty, project preparation, procurement, tariffs, and contract enforcement.
4. **Technology allocation** — how investment is distributed across grid, mini-grid, off-grid, fossil, hydro, geothermal, solar, wind, and storage.
5. **Equity and access** — whether investment reaches underserved communities and supports affordability and productive use.

### 2030 lens

The 2030 horizon provides a practical benchmark because it links energy access, NDC implementation, SDG 7, infrastructure investment, and broader sustainable-development goals.

The project will track whether policy commitments are being matched by capital flows and implementation outcomes.

### Quantitative agenda

Potential indicators include:

- annual energy investment by source and technology
- renewable capacity additions
- transmission and distribution investment
- electricity access and reliability
- clean-cooking access
- policy and regulatory scores
- private participation in infrastructure
- development-finance commitments
- sovereign and currency risk

R will be used for trend analysis, cross-country comparison, visualization, and regression-based exploration of the relationship between policy conditions and investment outcomes.

### Comparative cases

Priority cases may include Kenya, Ghana, Rwanda, South Africa, Morocco, and selected East African countries, depending on data quality and analytical purpose.

### Policy relevance

The work is intended to be useful for governments, AfDB, World Bank Group, regional institutions, climate-finance organizations, investors, and researchers working on the implementation gap between energy ambition and capital deployment.
