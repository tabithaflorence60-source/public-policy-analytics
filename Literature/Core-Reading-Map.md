# Core Reading Map

This literature map supports the research portfolio **Global Sustainable Investment & Energy Transition**. Readings are curated according to how directly they strengthen the research question, analytical framework, methods, or applied cases.

## Core literature

### Ahlborg et al. (2019) — Socio-Technical-Ecological Systems
**Why it matters:** Provides an interdisciplinary framework linking society, technology, and ecology and treats technology as a mediator of human–environment relationships through power, agency, scale, and system feedbacks.

**Use in this portfolio:** Supports the governance and sustainability-transition framework for examining renewable-energy infrastructure as embedded in social, technical, ecological, and institutional systems.

**Best fit:** Governance & Institutions; Energy Transitions; Kenya–Portugal comparative research.

**Key analytical use:** Helps move beyond treating renewable technologies as isolated technical assets by asking how infrastructure, institutions, environmental conditions, and power relations interact.

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.3390/su11072009

---

### Wojtaszek (2025) — Renewable Energy in Policy Frameworks: A Comparative Analysis of EU and Global Strategies for Sustainable Development
**Why it matters:** Compares EU and global renewable-energy strategies across policy goals, regulatory and financial instruments, governance mechanisms, and implementation barriers.

**Use in this portfolio:** Provides a useful comparative-policy precedent for Portugal/EU analysis and for developing an original framework around investment mobilization, governance coherence, implementation capacity, equity, and transition outcomes.

**Best fit:** Global Climate Policy; Governance & Institutions; Sustainable Investment; Comparative Energy Governance.

**Key analytical use:** Demonstrates how policy documents can be systematically coded and compared using transparent categories and triangulation with IEA, IRENA, IPCC, and secondary literature.

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.3390/su172310567

## How readings are classified

**CORE** — directly shapes the framework, research design, methods, or flagship research.

**APPLIED** — provides a useful case, dataset, policy instrument, empirical comparison, or teaching application.

**BACKGROUND** — useful for understanding the field but not central enough to feature prominently.

**SKIP** — does not materially strengthen the sustainable-investment, energy-policy, governance, or analytical niche.
