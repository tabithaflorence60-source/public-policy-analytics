# Who Governs Renewable-Energy Investment?

## Institutions, Regulation and the Politics of Implementation

### Research question

How do governance arrangements shape which renewable-energy investments move from policy commitment to implementation?

### Core argument

Renewable-energy investment is not governed by one institution. It is shaped by a network of ministries, regulators, utilities, public financiers, private investors, local authorities, communities, international organizations, and development banks. The quality of coordination across these actors can be as important as the formal policy itself.

### Governance dimensions

This project focuses on:

- regulatory authority and independence
- utility governance and financial viability
- procurement and contract credibility
- national-local coordination
- public-private partnerships
- multilateral and development-finance influence
- stakeholder participation and social legitimacy
- policy consistency across climate, energy, finance, and development institutions

### Comparative lens

The project uses comparative cases to examine how different institutional systems handle similar transition challenges.

**Kenya** provides an important case through geothermal development, electricity-sector institutions, public investment, development finance, and implementation around large infrastructure projects.

**Portugal and the EU** provide a contrasting setting involving EU climate policy, national energy regulation, regional and island systems, and multilevel governance.

The comparison is not intended to rank systems. It is intended to identify which governance functions enable or constrain investment and implementation.

### Analytical approach

The project combines:

- institutional mapping
- policy and regulatory analysis
- comparative case study methods
- interviews and documentary evidence
- investment and energy indicators

### Key questions

- Who has formal authority over investment decisions?
- Who controls finance, permitting, procurement, and implementation?
- Where do coordination failures occur?
- How do international institutions influence domestic choices?
- How are community interests and transition costs incorporated?
- Which governance arrangements reduce investor uncertainty without weakening accountability?
